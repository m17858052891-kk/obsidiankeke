                                                   Abstract

          Predictive models trained on observational data often fail to generalise to the
          distributions they encounter when deployed, especially when the training data is
          aproduct ofthe systembeing optimised. Recommendersystemsare acanonical
          example: theyare trainedoninteraction logsconfoundedby thedeployedpolicy,
          past user behaviour, and platform filtering. As a result, the training distribution
          differs substantially from thecandidate distribution scoredat serving time,a gap
          thatmakesofflinemetricsunreliablepredictorsofonlineperformance. Weaddress
          the distribution shift problem with a method motivated by causal representation
          learning (CRL). We propose an information-theoretic disentanglement criterion
          andprovethatitsoptimumexcludesconfounder-dependentnon-causalinformation
          from the treatment representation. We then derive a tractable variational lower
          bound that makes the criterion optimisable from finite observational data alone.
          The scope of our method is narrower than that of much of the CRL literature, in
          thatwetargetbettergeneralisationunderdistributionshift,notfullidentificationof
          alllatent causalfactors. This narrowertarget iswhatmakes themethodpractical,
          requiring only the existing confounded logs, applying to any standard supervised
          model, and adding no inference-time cost.  Our headline evaluation is an A/B
          test with millions of users on the music streaming platform Spotify, applied to a
          production ranker for personalised playlist generation. A CRL variant matched
          in capacity to the production baseline performed on par offline but delivered
          substantial online gains in listener engagement. Complementary evidence on a
          syntheticdataset withknown causalstructureand asemi-syntheticvariant ofthe
          public KuaiRand recommendation dataset shows the same pattern:  similar in-
          distribution performance to the baseline, gains under distribution shift. Across all
          three settings, adding our causal disentanglement objective yields better out-of-
          distribution generalisation.

1   Introduction

Predictive models deployed in feedback loops with the systems that produce their training data
face a basic problem: the distribution they are trained on diverges from the one they are asked
to score at serving time. In this work we focus on recommender systems, where the (user, item,
context) tuples appearing in training logs are those a deployed policy already chose to surface, and
arefurtherfilteredbyuserengagement. Thisphenomenon,broadlyreferredtoasexposurebiasinthe
recommendation literature Chen et al. [2023], means that at serving time the same model is asked
to score a much broader and differently distributed candidate set. The gap between training and

suppressed directly by enforcing that the learned representation carries no information about the

drives the offline-to-online gap, and it does so using only the existing observational training data. We

4.4   Discussion

Across all three settings, in-distribution performance remains unchanged while performance under
distributionshiftimproves. Thesyntheticandsemi-syntheticexperimentsdemonstraterobustness
toknownnon-causaltreatmentvariation,andtheproductionA/Btestdemonstratesonlinegainsin
listener engagement. Our theoretical analysis assumes that the relevant confounding information

is observed in Z.  The penalty cannot remove dependence transmitted only through unobserved
confounders. In production, the available user and session context almost surely does not include
all relevant confounding factors. The observed gains nevertheless suggest that an informative but
incompleteZ canstillimprovegeneralisation. Combiningthecriterionwithproximalcausalinference

5   Conclusion

We proposed a CRL-motivated method for making predictive models more robust under the distri-
bution shift characteristic of on-policy recommendation. We introduced an information-theoretic
disentanglement criterion, characterised its properties, and derived a tractable training objective. The
methodusesexistingobservationallogsandaddsnoinference-timecost. Syntheticandsemi-synthetic
experimentsdemonstrateimprovedrobustnessundercontrolledshifts,whilealarge-scaleproduction
A/B test showed significant gains in listener engagement.
