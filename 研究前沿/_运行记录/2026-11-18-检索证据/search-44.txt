## Abstract:

Time-aware sequential recommendation aims to predict which item a user will purchase, while temporal information helps characterize evolving user preferences and item dependencies based on historical interactions. Existing time-aware sequential recommendation models often use temporal information mainly as auxiliary features such as time intervals or cyclical patterns extracted from purchase records. In this work, we propose a recommendation-oriented perspective in which temporal signals are explicitly modeled as an additional learning objective to support item recommendation, rather than being used only as contextual features. Within a unified framework, our approach jointly learns next-item prediction together with the expected time of the next interaction, where item recommendation remains the primary objective and temporal prediction provides explicit temporal supervision for representation learning and reranking. Specifically, we propose a unified Item–Time Dual Recommendation framework named ITDRec. Built on a standard time-aware sequential backbone, ITDRec jointly learns next-item prediction and candidate-conditioned future time estimation within a unified architecture. To support this formulation, ITDRec employs a time decoder with a candidate-aware Chronological Preference Mapping (CPM) module based on cross-attention, together with three complementary time predictors for modeling temporal signals from multiple perspectives. Extensive experiments on multiple real-world datasets demonstrate that ITDRec consistently and significantly outperforms strong baselines on item recommendation. Additional results on time prediction show that the temporal branch learns meaningful temporal patterns that provide useful signals for the recommendation task.

## Abstract

Sequential recommendation is one of the hot research topics in recent years. Various sequential recommendation models have been proposed, of which Self-Attention (SA)-based models are shown to have state-of-the-art performance. However, most of the existing SA-based sequential recommendation models do not make use of temporal information, i.e., timestamps of user–item interactions, except for an initial attempt (Li et al., 2020). In this paper, we propose a Time-Aware Transformer for Sequential Recommendation (TAT4SRec), an SA-based neural network model which utilizes the temporal information and captures users’ preferences more precisely. TAT4SRec has two salient features: (1) TAT4SRec utilizes an encoder–decoder structure to model timestamps and interacted items separately and this structure appears to be a better way of making use of the temporal information. (2) in the proposed TAT4SRec, two different embedding modules are designed to transform continuous data (timestamps) and discrete data (item IDs) into embedding matrices respectively. Specifically, we propose a window function-based embedding module to preserve the continuous dependency contained in similar timestamps. Finally, extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed TAT4SRec over various state-of-the-art MC/RNN/SA-based sequential recommendation models under several widely-used metrics. Furthermore, experiments are also performed to show the rationality of the different proposed structures and demonstrate the computation efficiency of TAT4SRec. The promising experimental results make it possible to apply TAT4SRec in various online applications.

Moreover, SA-based models enjoy a faster computational speed than MC/RNN-based models because self-attention mechanism is suitable for parallel acceleration [14], [21]. Due to the above benefits, recent models in sequential recommender systems are predominantly based on self-attention mechanism with various architectures like SASRec [14] and FDSA [20].

However, in our research, we observe that most of the existing SA-based sequential recommendation models have one common drawback, i.e., they do not make use of the temporal information (i.e., timestamps of user–item interactions). Most existing SA-based sequential recommendation models only use the order of each element in the user–item interaction sequence by introducing positional embedding [14], [17]. We argue that by incorporating the timestamps, an SA-based sequential recommendation model may capture more complicated sequential dependencies over the users’ historical interaction sequence, therefore may achieve better model performance. The above argument is quite straightforward, and here we give a simple example to illustrate it. Fig. 1 shows a record (sequence) of a user’s interaction on an e-commerce website, the n axis shows the order of the items and the t axis shows the timestamps of user–item interactions. It can be seen that in this specific example, if we only consider the order of items (the n axis), the intervals between items are equal; whereas if we consider the interaction time intervals (the t axis), the intervals are different. Obviously the adoption of both the order and temporal information may help a SA-based model to better capture a user’s behavior patterns.

In this paper, we propose a Time-Aware Transformer for Sequential Recommendation (TAT4SRec), a new SA-based sequential recommendation model involving temporal information (timestamps). Our model has the following salient features: (1) The proposed TAT4SRec makes use of the temporal information (timestamps) in the model. To the best of our knowledge, in existing SA-based sequential recommendation models, only TiSASRec [1] uses temporal information, which was a nice attempt in related research. 
Empirical studies are conducted to show the characteristics of TAT4SRec under different hyper-parameter settings and explore the compact of different key components in the proposed TAT4SRec. Furthermore, we conduct comparative experiments on two datasets used widely in related research [1], [22], [23], [24]. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed TAT4SRec outperforms existing sequential recommendation models including MC/RNN/SA-based models consistently.

The main contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows:

  * •

We propose a Time-Aware Transformer for Sequential Recommendation (TAT4SRec), which incorporates temporal information to better capture users’ preferences.

  * •

For the proposed TAT4SRec, an encoder–decoder structure is designed to process timestamps and interacted items respectively. This structure of modeling timestamps and interacted items separately is shown to utilizes temporal information better than existing method [1].

  * •

We propose a window function-based embedding module for timestamps, which can better preserve the continuous characteristics of timestamps and can be applicable when introducing other continuous data (e.g., the price of items)

  * •

The encoder–decoder structure proposed in this paper provides an alternative direction for future sequential recommendation models to introduce other side information (e.g., product categories, reviews, locations, etc.)

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. A series of related works are introduced in Section 2. Section 3 presents the definitions and details of the TAT4SRec. Section 4 reports the results of the experiments on evaluation of the effectiveness and computational efficiency of our model. Conclusions and future work are drawn in Section 5.

Large-scale recommenders increasingly adopt the sequential generative recipe behind large language models, bringing the Transformer into recommendation along with design choices made for text, including Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE). In language models, RoPE encodes token indices for relative position reasoning, but in recommendation, an interaction index records only event order, saying nothing about elapsed time, behavioral cycles across scales, or calendar phase. We revisit this choice and propose T-RoPE, a time-aware RoPE for sequential generative recommendation that replaces index-only rotation with timestamp-based angles, learnable temporal coefficients, multiscale frequency banks, shifted query alignment, and non-stationary key rotation. # Flow-based Time-aware Causal Structure Learning for Sequential Recommendation

## Hangtong Xu, Yuanbo Xu, Huayuan Liu, En Wang

Proceedings of the Thirty-Fourth International Joint Conference on Artificial Intelligence

Main Track. Pages 3498-3506. https://doi.org/10.24963/ijcai.2025/389

PDF BibTeX

Sequential models aim to predict future interactions based on users' historical interaction sequences. Traditional sequential methods primarily focus on capturing intra-historical sequence dependencies, overlooking the influence of unobserved confounders in recommendation scenarios. Recent studies incorporate time as additional information helps the model capture dynamic user preferences. However, time is just the external manifestation of the influence of confounders but not the actual cause of the dynamic of user preference. Additionally, improperly integrating time with item embeddings can obstruct the model's ability to capture sequence dependencies. To address these challenges, we first revisit the sequential recommendation problem from a causal perspective and incorporate confounders as a new task. We propose a new framework—Flow-based Time-aware Causal Structure for Sequential Recommendation (FCSRec)—explicitly incorporating unobserved confounders' influence in the recommendation process. Specifically, we use Normalizing Flows to learn the causal graph of confounders and incorporate time information as conditional info to capture confounders' time-sensitive representations. To balance the influence of confounders and sequence dependencies, we introduce a classifier-free training paradigm by randomly masking the influence of confounders during training to encourage the model to learn both sequence dependencies and confounders' influence equally. We validate FCSRec on manifold real-world datasets, and experimental results show that FCSRec outperforms several state-of-the-art methods in recommendation performance.
