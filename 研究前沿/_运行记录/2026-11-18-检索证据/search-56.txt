#### 3.1.3. Sequential Request Rejection Process

Ride-hailing requests are processed through a sequential dispatch mechanism in which a passenger request may be offered to multiple drivers before a successful driver–passenger match is established. The present study focuses specifically on requests that ultimately result in successful matching. Accordingly, the unit of analysis is an eventually matched passenger request, rather than an individual accept–reject decision made by a single driver. Requests that remain unmatched or are canceled before successful acceptance are outside the analytical scope of the study. Within this defined population, the sequential dispatch history of each request is characterized by the number of unsuccessful driver assignments preceding eventual acceptance and the time required to reach that successful match.

The first request-level outcome, count of rejects, is defined as the number of consecutive driver rejections experienced by a passenger request before it is ultimately accepted. A value of zero indicates that the request is accepted by the first driver to whom it is dispatched. A positive value indicates that one or more drivers reject the request before a subsequent driver accepts it. Thus, the rejection count represents the accumulated sequential rejection exposure of the passenger request prior to successful matching, rather than repeated rejection behavior by a single driver.

The second request-level outcome, matching time, captures the temporal consequence of this sequential process from the passenger perspective. It is defined as the elapsed duration between the creation of passenger request i and its eventual acceptance by a driver:

$${\mathit{MT}}_{i}= {t}_{i}^{\mathit{accept}}-{t}_{i}^{\mathit{request}}$$

(4)

where ${t}_{i}^{\mathit{request}}$ denotes the time at which passenger request i is created on the platform and ${t}_{i}^{\mathit{accept}}$ denotes the time at which the same request is ultimately accepted. Requests accepted at the first dispatch therefore have zero rejection exposure and immediate or near-immediate matching, whereas requests experiencing one or more unsuccessful assignments generally remain in the matching process for a longer period before acceptance. --------------------------------------------------------------------------------
