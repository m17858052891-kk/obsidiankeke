- [S1.F1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04870v1#S1.F1)：Figure 1 : PrimeSim. (a) The midtraining corpus: roughly 30.1K hours of curated robot data spanning humanoid, dual-arm, single-arm and handheld UMI [ 14 , 3 ] platforms, mixing real and simulated footage. (b) The model is conditioned on a language prompt, a unified action representation over head, torso, waist, end-effector, gripper and chassis, and three camera views, and predicts future frames; post-training makes generation autoregressive with a selected KV-cache memory at four denoising steps. (c) Capabilities: fidelity against prior systems, action following measured against simulation ground truth, few-shot adaptation to an unseen embodiment, and use as a simulator: success rate in PrimeSim predicts success rate in the LIBERO [ 51 ] physics simulator with r = 0.99 r=0.99 , so policies can be trained in the model and the result carried back to the robot.

Each stage lists the operations applied to the incoming data. The pipeline produces a unified corpus of 30,110 hours across 20 dataset families and 22 robot platforms (Table 12 ).

- [S4.F7](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04870v1#S4.F7)：(a) xArm 7 , midtrained, 200 gradient steps. (b) xArm 7 , pretrained backbone, 2000 gradient steps. (c) ViperX 300 , midtrained, 100 gradient steps. (d) ViperX 300 , pretrained backbone, 2000 gradient steps. (e) Original Stanford UMI gripper [ 14 ] , midtrained, 200 gradient steps. (f) Original Stanford UMI gripper [ 14 ] , pretrained backbone, 2000 gradient steps. Figure 7 : Few-shot adaptation to unseen embodiments. Each pair shows the midtrained model above the pretrained backbone for one platform held out from training: a single 7-DoF arm ( xArm 7 ) drawn from Open X-Embodiment [ 17 ] , a bimanual platform ( ViperX 300 , as used by ALOHA [ 98 ] ) and the original Stanford UMI gripper [ 14 ] . Similar embodiments do occur in the corpus, and they are what make transfer possible at all, since a wholly alien platform would leave nothing to build on. They are still new embodiments, however, and adaptation between embodiments does not happen for free: each brings its own kinematics, camera mounting and data distribution, which have to be acquired rather than inherited. All initialisations are finetuned under an identical recipe with single-trajectory batches at a batch size of 1, and none receives an embodiment identifier or any embodiment-specific parameters. The midtrained model reaches an action-following rollout within 100 to 200 gradient steps on all three platforms. The pretrained backbone, which must initialise the action-conditioning and multiview modules from scratch, does not reach the same regime even at ten to twenty times that budget.
